How a phone is found

This is how location data works in general; the numbers in the scene are invented. A phone does not need to be tracked by a camera. Apps on it report where it is.

  • GPSThe phone listens to satellites and works out its own position, usually within a few meters outdoors.
  • Wi-Fi and BluetoothNearby networks and beacons are matched against a known map, which works indoors where GPS does not.
  • Cell towers (triangulation)Signal strength and timing to two or three towers narrow the phone to an area, often tens to hundreds of meters.
  • Apps report itA weather or game app asks for location once. A code library inside it keeps sending the position, a timestamp and the phone's advertising ID to a data broker.

That is why the matching in step 1 works: a broker's file is a long list of "this advertising ID was here at this time," and a plate sighting is "this car was here at this time."

What a phone reveals that a car does not

  • Home and workWhere the phone sits overnight and through the workday, down to the building.
  • Regular visitsA clinic, a place of worship, a union hall, a bar, once or every week.
  • Life on footTrips the car never made: a walk, a bus ride, a friend's house.
  • Who else is therePhones that keep turning up in the same place at the same time.
  • ChangesA new overnight address, a long stay at a hospital, a sudden stop in a routine.

Add records that share a key

Once one advertising ID is tied to the plate, other purchased files that use the same ID, phone number or name can attach contact details, household members and shopping habits. Each file only needs to share one key with another.

What would limit this

  • Shorter retentionOn reads and on purchased location data: fewer sightings to line up.
  • Warrant or court-order rulesFor searching reads and for buying data about people.
  • Limits on sharingOutside the agency that owns the camera, and on who may search.
  • Search logsWith a specific reason and a case number, reviewed and published.
  • Vendor limitsNot collecting or selling precise phone locations in the first place.
